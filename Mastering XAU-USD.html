What To Study
1. Price Action / Market Structure

Higher highs & higher lows (uptrend) vs lower highs & lower lows (downtrend). Break of structure (BOS) and change of character (CHoCH) — how trend shifts get confirmed. Support/resistance as zones, not exact lines.

2. Key Levels That Matter for Gold

Previous day's high/low (PDH/PDL). Asian session high/low. Round numbers ($3400, $3450, $3500 — gold respects these). Weekly open/high/low.

3. Macro Drivers — Gold's Real Engine

US 10-year real yields (gold moves inverse to real yields). DXY / Dollar Index (gold is usually inverse to dollar strength). Risk sentiment — safe-haven flows during uncertainty. Fed policy expectations — rate cut/hike odds move gold hard.

4. The Economic Calendar

NFP, CPI, FOMC statements/rate decisions, PCE — know exactly when they land each week. Understand direction of surprise (e.g. hot CPI = hawkish = often gold down).

Entries To Master
Trend-Aligned Pullback (Scalp)

Higher-timeframe EMA8/21/50 stacked → pullback to EMA21 → rejection candle with rising volume → enter on candle close or break of its high/low.

Break-and-Retest (Day Trade)

Price breaks a key level (PDH/PDL, round number, Asian range) → retest of that level → rejection candle in the direction of the break, aligned with daily bias.

Liquidity Sweep + Reversal (Day Trade)

Price wicks beyond a key level (sweeping stops) then snaps back through it → wait for M15 structure confirmation (BOS) before entering the reversal.

Non-Negotiable Filters

Trend filter — only trade in the direction of higher-timeframe structure. Tangled/flat EMAs = no trade.

News filter — no entries within 15-30 min of NFP/CPI/FOMC/PCE.

Session filter — London open and London-NY overlap only. Asian session gold is typically choppy.

Risk filter — 0.5-1% risk per trade, stop beyond structure, max daily loss limit (e.g. -3%).

Study Plan

Weeks 3-4: Demo trade live, London/NY sessions only, journal every trade against the checklist.

Step-by-Step: Marking Zones & Stacking Confluence

No confluence has a fixed "win rate" — that depends on your exact rules and sample size, not the factor itself. What follows are the highest-quality, most structurally sound confluences to stack — not a guarantee, a filter for setup quality.

Step 1 — Mark your zones every morning

Previous day's high/low (PDH/PDL). Asian session high/low. Nearest round numbers ($50 increments — $3400, $3450, $3500). Weekly open. Draw these as zones (a small band, not a single line) — price rarely respects an exact pixel.

Step 2 — Rank the zones by confluence density

A zone where 2-3 of the above overlap (e.g. PDH sits right on a round number) is structurally stronger than one with just one factor. Prioritize trading these "stacked" zones over isolated ones.

Step 3 — Require trend agreement

The zone means little if it fights the higher-timeframe trend. Only take a long reaction at a zone if H1/H4 structure or EMA stack is also bullish (same logic in reverse for shorts). This is the single biggest quality filter you have.

Step 4 — Require macro agreement (when available)

Check DXY direction. If your zone says long and DXY is breaking down at the same time, that's a third layer of confluence. If DXY is breaking out against your trade direction, treat that as a warning, not something to ignore.

Step 5 — Wait for a confirmed price-action trigger at the zone

Don't enter because price "reached" the zone — wait for a rejection candle, a break-and-retest, or a liquidity sweep + structure break (BOS) to actually confirm the zone is holding. The zone is where you look, not where you click buy.

Step 6 — Score the setup before entering

A quick mental checklist: zone confluence (1-3 factors stacked) + trend agreement (yes/no) + macro agreement (yes/no/neutral) + confirmed trigger (yes/no). The more of these stacked, the higher-quality the setup — trade the A-grade setups, skip the ones with only one factor.

This scoring system is a discipline tool, not a formula with a fixed edge. Its real value only shows up once you've backtested it yourself and can see, from your own journal, which combinations actually held up on XAUUSD.
